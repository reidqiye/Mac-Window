Why a game?
From passive understanding to active participation

In the early stages of my research, I focused on how AI records, interprets, and reuses employees’ work knowledge. I drew on academic papers and real-world cases to examine how that knowledge is digitised and how AI systems extract it.

When AI turns employees’ actions at work into reusable representations of their knowledge, what tension arises between employees’ control over their own work knowledge and the organisation’s authority to manage it?

This question led me to consider which medium could best communicate my research. A film, an article, or a static visual work would primarily let an audience observe and understand the process. In a game, players take part in it. As they carry out tasks, they experience how the system records, interprets, and reuses the knowledge they draw on.

In the game I envision, the player takes on the role of a company employee. The system assigns repetitive tasks, and the player makes judgements within a prescribed workflow. As the player works, the system logs their actions and classification decisions and gradually turns them into data it can store and use.

I also want to use the game mechanics to explore tacit knowledge. At work, rules and documentation can capture only part of what employees know from experience. Much of that knowledge is embedded in how employees carry out tasks and make judgements in specific situations.

In the game, the system tries to turn that experience into an employee profile and AI-generated recommendations by logging the player’s actions and decisions. The resulting profile and recommendations reflect what the system can capture, classify, and interpret. They may therefore give an incomplete account of the employee’s experience.

Four aspects of the game make this tension tangible.
- Participation. By taking on the role of an employee, the player participates in producing knowledge as the system records it.
- Repetition. Repeating the same work tasks lets the player experience the mechanical routine of digital labour as the system accumulates data on their actions.
- Agency. The player makes decisions within each task. Their control over how the system records, interprets, and uses those decisions is limited.
- Consequences. After leaving the company, the player can still view their profile but can no longer edit it. Employees who join later may still draw on the experience captured in that profile.
I chose a game because its mechanics let players experience the power relations at the centre of my research. Players have room to decide how they carry out tasks and what judgements they make. Their control over the data and knowledge representations those actions produce may be more limited.

Through that experience, I want players to consider a question. Who owns an employee’s work knowledge once it becomes a resource the organisation can keep using?

网页不仅是载体，也是作品所讨论的工作环境

选择以网页的形式不光是因为其传播与随地游玩的便捷性。

我所研究的主要工作场景是当代数字化环境，而网页本身就是许多企业管理系统、在线劳动平台和员工数据库的常见界面形式。

因此，网页不是一个用来展示故事的容器，而是故事发生的环境本身。

比如说一些界面可以设定为：
Familiar Interface： 登录、按钮、数据库和员工档案让玩家自然地进入一个模拟企业系统。
System Control： 玩家只能通过界面提供的操作和权限与系统互动。
Data Persistence： 数据被保存在服务端，玩家结束操作后，相关档案仍然存在。
Accessibility： 无需安装专门软件，用户可以直接通过浏览器体验作品。

## Why make it web-based?
*The web as both platform and workplace*

I chose to make the project web-based because the browser is already part of the digital workplace I am studying. It also makes the game easy to share and lets players access it without installing software.

My research focuses on how AI systems record, manage, and reuse employees’ work knowledge in contemporary digital workplaces. Many company management systems, online work platforms, and employee databases are already accessed through web interfaces. I wanted the game to look and behave like one of those workplace systems.

Players enter this simulated workplace by logging in, completing tasks, and viewing employee profiles. These familiar interactions help them settle into the role of an employee and learn how the system works as they use it.

- **Familiar interface.** A login screen, a work dashboard, a database, and employee profiles give the game the structure of a digital workplace.
- **System control.** The system defines the workflow and limits what players can do. Their permissions determine which information they can view, edit, or use.
- **Data persistence.** A server-side database stores players’ work records and profiles after their sessions end. Employees who join later can use those records.
- **Accessibility.** Players can open the game directly in a browser without installing dedicated software, making it easier to take part.

I wanted the web-based design to make the difference between data retention and access permissions tangible. **When players leave the company, their permissions change. They can lose the ability to edit their own profiles while the system continues to store and use the records they created at work.**

The browser interface puts that change in front of the player. Through a familiar dashboard, a database that retains records, and permissions that change when employment ends, I want players to experience the imbalance between the people whose labour produces the data and the organisation that controls its use.

### Gameplay Flow — 游戏流程

**01. Login — 入职**

玩家首先注册或登录企业系统，并以员工身份开始工作。游戏没有传统的故事开场，而是直接将玩家放入一个模拟的数字工作环境中。

**02. Work — 工作与数据收集**

玩家需要完成公司分配的任务。

在重复工作中，玩家逐渐形成自己的操作习惯和判断方式，而系统则持续记录他们的分类结果、错误、暂存行为和工作表现。

与此同时，系统可以利用前员工留下的数据生成 AI Advice（建议），并通过演示其操作方式，让过去员工的经验继续影响当前玩家。

03.资料的完善

随着工作记录的积累，系统逐渐生成玩家自己的员工档案。

档案包含工作统计、行为记录以及 AI 生成的个人介绍。原本属于玩家个人的操作习惯和经验，被整理为企业可以保存和使用的数字信息。

游戏的胜利进度条会随着数据收集度的上升而上升。

**04. Profile Complete — 知识记录完成**

当系统判定员工档案已经完成，玩家将进入 Profile Complete 阶段。

此时，系统认为已经完成对该员工工作知识的记录，玩家作为劳动者的价值开始被其数字档案所替代。

系统终止其雇佣状态。

**05.  Employee Profile

玩家失去了员工身份，但其工作数据、个人档案和 AI 生成的知识仍然保存在系统中，可以继续为企业提供价值。

玩家可以查看系统保存的历史员工档案。

当玩家退出并重新登录时，他们不再能够进入原来的工作系统，而是进入前员工专属界面。

玩家仍然可以查看自己的 Archived Profile（归档档案），但无法对其进行修改。

当玩家尝试点击 Edit 时，系统会显示 Access Denied（无权限）。

玩家已经失去了对自身工作知识的控制权，而企业仍然保留并使用这些数据。

**06. — 员工档案生成**

这些档案展示了员工的工作数据和被系统整理后的经验。即使员工已经离开，他们留下的信息仍然可以被企业使用，为后续员工提供参考与建议。

### 01. Login (onboarding)

The player creates an account or logs in to the company’s system and starts work as an employee. The game opens directly in the simulated digital workplace, with the player’s first interaction taking place through the company system.

### 02. Work (tasks and data collection)

The player completes tasks assigned by the company. As they repeat these tasks, they develop their own working habits and ways of making judgements. The system keeps a record of how they classify items, what errors they make, which items they set aside for later, and how they perform.

The system can also use data left by former employees to generate **AI Advice**. By demonstrating how those employees handled tasks, it lets their experience continue to shape how the current player works.

### 03. Building the employee profile

As work records accumulate, the system builds the player’s employee profile. It contains performance statistics, a log of the player’s actions, and an AI-generated bio. The system turns the player’s individual working habits and experience into digital information the company can store and use.

The game’s progress bar towards victory fills as the system collects more data.

### 04. Profile Complete (knowledge capture)

The player reaches **Profile Complete** when the system determines that their employee profile is complete. From the system’s perspective, the employee’s work knowledge has now been captured, and the digital profile begins to take the employee’s place as a source of value for the company.

The system then terminates the player’s employment.

### 05. Employee Profile

After the player loses their employee status, the system retains their work data, employee profile, and the knowledge it generated from their records. The company can continue to use all three. The player can also browse the profiles of former employees stored in the system.

If the player logs out and signs back in, the system takes them to a screen for former employees. They can view their **Archived Profile** in read-only mode. Clicking **Edit** brings up **Access Denied**.

The player has lost control over the work knowledge stored in their profile. The company retains the data and continues to use it.

### 06. Generating employee profiles

These profiles show employees’ work data and how the system has organised their experience. After employees leave, the company can draw on their records to give later employees examples and advice.

## Visual style
*Neo-Brutalist Corporate Interface Design*

I chose a neo-brutalist corporate interface for **CONTINUUM**. Its visual qualities appeal to me, and the style supports the project’s exploration of digital labour, knowledge management, and corporate power.

High-contrast colours, thick borders, a grid layout, and a clear visual hierarchy give the interface an ordered, efficient appearance. That sense of order reflects how the company system operates in the game. It defines the workflow, records employee behaviour, and controls access to information.

Numbered entries, modular layouts, and standardised information structures also reflect how employees’ work knowledge is digitised. As players work, the system turns their habits, judgements, and experience into data it can classify, record, and manage.

I use black, white, and deep charcoal grey as the base palette, with acid yellow-green to draw attention to important actions, system statuses, and feedback. The restrained palette helps the game feel like a workplace terminal.

I want the interface to appear professional, clear, and efficient at first, so players can settle into the workflow and take its rules for granted. The pressure of the system emerges through play. As players carry out their tasks, they gradually realise that the seemingly neutral system is recording, interpreting, and reusing the experience they gain at work.

**The interface makes that transformation visible.** Its numbered entries and standardised fields give a concrete form to the way individual labour becomes data that the organisation can manage and use.
